# Domain Glossary

The vocabulary this codebase uses, and the reasoning behind the parts that aren't obvious. Terms here should match the names in `src/forecast/score.js` exactly.

---

## Core concepts

**Activity**
One of two modes of ocean use, Swimming or Freediving. Each Activity gets its own Verdict from its own tuning profile.

**Verdict**
The answer the app gives for one Activity at one time and place. Four states: *Go*, *Sketchy*, *No*, *Unknown*. Never a number. The number stays internal.

**Go**
Conditions suit the Activity. Proceed with confidence.

**Sketchy**
Conditions are marginal, the app recommends caution, and the user makes the call. This state exists because a hard binary misses the boundary that trained athletes actually navigate most days.

**No**
Conditions are unsuitable. Not a suggestion, a stop.

**Unknown**
The marine model has no coverage here, so there is nothing to judge. Distinct from *No*, because it is the absence of an answer rather than a negative one. See [ADR 0003](docs/adr/0003-unknown-verdict.md). It ranks below *No* everywhere verdicts get compared, so an hour the app cannot judge never looks better than one it judged and rejected.

**Score**
An internal 0 to 100 value computed from marine and weather signals, mapped to a Verdict by per-Activity thresholds. Never shown to the user, because a number invites arguing with it. Unknown carries `null` rather than a score.

**Limiting Factor**
Whichever single component, Swell or Chop or Wind, cost the most points this hour. The summary sentence and the detail-sheet bars are both generated from it, which is what stops the words and the chart from disagreeing.

## Ocean signals

**Wave Height**
Significant Wave Height from the Open-Meteo Marine API, meaning the average of the highest one-third of waves. Around 15% of individual waves equal or exceed it and the largest may be roughly twice it. This is the only raw number the app puts on screen, because it is the one metric experienced users already reason in.

**Swell**
Long-period waves generated by distant storms, carried in `swell_wave_height` and `swell_wave_period`. A period above 12s means waves travel faster and roll through more smoothly, which for Swimming is easier going than chop of the same height. For Freediving the same long period produces Surge at depth.

**Chop**
Short-period, locally wind-generated waves, carried in `wind_wave_height` and `wind_wave_period`. Chop is weighted more heavily than equivalent-height Swell for both Activities because it is more disruptive: it destroys stroke efficiency for Swimming and makes surface breath-recovery dangerous for Freediving. A choppy 0.4 m sea is worse than a swelly 0.4 m sea.

Chop is also *derived from the wind field* by Open-Meteo, which is why Wind is scored as a modifier rather than as an independent term. See [ADR 0002](docs/adr/0002-additive-scoring-model.md).

**Surge**
The back-and-forth underwater motion driven by wave period. Freediving only. Noticeable movement persists to roughly T²/4 metres, so an 8s period reaches about 16 m, a 12s period about 36 m, a 15s period about 56 m. The app computes this continuously rather than in bands. A long-period swell can look smooth from the beach and still produce real surge at recreational depths, which makes it the one hazard the surface does not advertise.

**Onshore, Offshore, Cross-shore**
Where the wind blows relative to the coast. *Offshore* runs land to sea: it grooms the surface flat and improves visibility, and past a threshold it starts pushing swimmers and stationary freedivers away from shore. *Onshore* runs sea to land, stacking up the shorebreak and stirring sediment. *Cross-shore* is everything in between.

The app has no coastline data, so it infers the shoreline's orientation from the bearing the swell arrives from. The heuristic and its failure mode are documented in [ADR 0002](docs/adr/0002-additive-scoring-model.md).

**Gust Factor**
`windgusts_10m` divided by `windspeed_10m`. Above about 1.5 the wind is squally, which is unpleasant in ways the mean speed does not capture.

**Cold Shock**
Water below 13 °C, where involuntary gasp reflex is a hazard whatever the user is wearing. It caps the Score. Merely cold water between 13 and 19 °C does not, because that is a wetsuit decision rather than a Verdict.

## Time concepts

**Go Window**
A run of consecutive hours in a forecast day where one Activity's Verdict is Go. Minimum two hours. Computed per Activity: requiring both Activities to agree used to hide flawless swimming days that happened to be surge-y for freediving.

**Best Window**
The longest Go Window in a day, shown as a chip on that Activity's card for future days. A window spanning the whole day renders as "all day" rather than "12am–midnight".

**Morning Briefing**
A planned daily push notification at 6am summarising conditions for every Saved Spot. One per day per location, maximum.

**Improvement Alert**
A planned push notification fired when a Saved Spot flips to Go and at least a two-hour Go Window lies ahead. At most once per day per location, however much conditions fluctuate.

---

## Location concepts

**Saved Spot**
A location saved by the user, identified by coordinates and a display name, persisted to local storage. The primary spots are in Israel, around Tel Aviv beach and the Caesarea coastline, but the app works anywhere the marine model has coverage.

**Cam**
A live beach webcam tied to a Saved Spot or searched location, surfaced as a button in the Detail View. The button only appears where a cam is known. Not wired up yet.

## UI terms

**Conditions Bar**
Three at-a-glance readings above the Verdict Cards: wave height, water temperature, and wind speed with an arrow pointing the way it blows. The arrow rotates by bearing plus 180°, because meteorological convention reports where wind comes *from* and a person on the sand is picturing where it's going.

**Verdict Card**
One of two equal cards on the home screen, Swimming on top and Freediving below. Each carries an Activity label, the Verdict word, a Best Window chip on future days, and a one-line Condition Summary. Tap to expand.

**Condition Summary**
One plain-English sentence explaining why the Verdict came out that way, generated from the Limiting Factor. For example "Wind chop makes surface recovery harder between dives." Never uses raw variable names.

**Detail View**
The expanded state of a Verdict Card. Wave height as a large number, the Condition Summary, a three-bar breakdown showing how much of each factor's quality survived, and supporting chips for water temperature, wind relation, gusts, and swell wavelength. Draws no bars at all when the Verdict is Unknown.

**Day Selector**
A horizontal strip of seven day pills, each with a label and a coloured dot showing the best Verdict reachable that day across both Activities. Tapping a day updates everything below it.

**Hourly Strip**
A scrollable bar chart of the selected day. Bar height encodes score, colour encodes Verdict, and every third hour is labelled. It replaced a row of dots that abbreviated "Sketchy" to "Skchy" to make it fit.

**Ambient Tint**
A soft column of colour rising behind the conditions screen, tinted by the better of the two Verdicts. Unknown gets no tint, since there is nothing to signal.
